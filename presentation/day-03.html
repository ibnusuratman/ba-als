What is Annotating?

It is often confused with Note-taking. Here is the difference:

Annotating 🖍️ Note-Taking 📝
Writing ON the text (Highlight, Underline, Margin notes). Writing OUTSIDE the text (Notebook, Laptop).
Goal: Interactive dialogue with the author while reading. Goal: Summarizing and synthesizing after reading.

The "Yellow Disease"

Critique of Over-Highlighting:

Many students highlight entire sentences. This is wrong!

  • ❌ If everything is highlighted, nothing is important.
  • ❌ It clutters the page and makes review difficult.
  • ✅ Golden Rule: Only highlight NEW information. Do not highlight what you already know!

Strategy 1: Keywords & Margins

Focus on the "Meat", not the "Bone".

1. Selectivity

  • Don't highlight: "The program consists of five on-site modules..."
  • Do highlight: "5 modules", "2 weeks each".

2. Marginal Notes

  • Write abbreviations in the margin (e.g., use "4" instead of "four").
  • Write questions for the professor (e.g., "Why?").

Strategy 2: Double-Entry Journal

Used for Descriptive Texts (e.g., Havasu Canyon).

Split your paper into two columns:

From the Text (Facts) From Your Mind (Reaction)
"Villagers stare without smiling." "They seem unfriendly/scary."
"Waterfalls drop into pools." "Sounds magical/beautiful."

Benefit: Turns passive reading into active dialogue.

Strategy 3: Annotating Islamic Texts

Special rules apply for IOU students.

What to Highlight:

  • ✅ Ayat & Hadith: The core evidence (Dalil).
  • ✅ Terminology: E.g., Definition of Nikah (Linguistic vs Technical).
  • ✅ Rulings (Ahkam): Wajib, Haram, Valid, Invalid.

What NOT to Highlight:

  • ❌ General Knowledge: E.g., "We need food to survive".

Case Study: The Wali (Guardian)

Applying annotation to Fiqh Rulings.

  • Evidence: Highlight the Hadith "No marriage is valid without a wali".
  • Hierarchy: Father ➔ Grandfather ➔ Brother ➔ Uncle.
  • Prohibition: A woman CANNOT marry herself off (Invalid).
  • Mistake: Appointing a friend as Wali is haram and leads to Khalwat.
