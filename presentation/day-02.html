MODULE 2

Vocabulary Development

Word Structure: The "Lego" Concept

Words are built from pieces. Guess meanings without a dictionary!

  • Root: The base meaning. (e.g., Photo, Clear)
  • Prefix: Added at the BEGINNING. Changes meaning.
    (e.g., Un-clear = Not clear)
  • Suffix: Added at the END. Changes grammar/form.
    (e.g., Clear-ly = Adverb)

Tip: Use Flashcards on your bedroom wall!

⚠️ Common Confusing Prefixes

These appear in quizzes often. Don't mix them up!

Prefix Meaning Examples (Variants)
Ante- Before / In front Antechamber (waiting room)
Antenatal (before birth)
Anti- Against Anticoagulant (prevents clotting)
Anticlockwise (reverse direction)
Hyper- Excessive Hyperactive, Hypermarket
Hypo- Under / Less Hypothermia (low body heat)

Suffixes & Compound Words

Suffix Examples

  • -ose (Full of): Adipose (fatty tissue).
  • -osis (Condition): Psychosis.
  • -cide (Killer): Pesticide.
  • -ous (Full of): Nauseous.

Compound Words

  • Closed: Football, Pacemaker.
  • Hyphenated: Mother-in-law.
  • Open: Post office.

Homophones: Same Sound, Diff Meaning

Memorize these pairs! Favorites for exam questions.

Affect (Verb: Influence) Effect (Noun: Result)
Principal (Head of School) Principle (Moral/Rule)
Capital (City/Money) Capitol (Building)

Context Clues: Guessing Game

Don't stop reading when you see a hard word. Look for clues:

  1. Definition: The sentence explains it.
    "Homogeneous, which means uniform..."
  2. Synonym: A similar word is nearby.
    "He was exalted, overjoyed by the news."
  3. Antonym: A contrast word.
    "Unlike his tentative brother, he was willing."

MODULE 3

Reading Strategies

Battle: Scanning vs. Skimming

Scanning (The Hunter) 🦅 Skimming (The Tourist) 🚌
Goal: Find SPECIFIC info (dates, numbers). Goal: Get GENERAL idea / Overview.
Action: Rapid eye movement for keywords. Action: Read Title, Headings, First/Last lines.
Example: Checking a price in a catalog. Example: Reading headlines.

Anatomy of a Journal Article

  • Abstract: Short summary (Read this first!).
  • Introduction: Background & Goal.
  • Methods: How research was done.
  • Results: The Findings.
  • Discussion: Interpretation.

Remember: Topic Sentence is usually the FIRST sentence of a paragraph.

The Ultimate Weapon: SQ3R

Move info from Short-term to Long-term Memory.

S

Survey

Q

Question

R

Read

R

Recite

R

Review

Example (History Class):

  • Q: Turn title "Minoan Society" into "What is Minoan Society?"
  • R: Read to find the answer (Crete Island).
  • Recite: Say aloud: "Minoan = Crete."
